Hunter.io vs sitetrace for email verification

They do very different things and we should start by saying so. Hunter finds email addresses and verifies individual mailboxes. We check the domain's authentication posture (SPF/DKIM/DMARC). Sometimes you need both.

Where sitetrace wins

"My customers' emails are landing in spam"

This is the deliverability problem. A SaaS or ESP wants to monitor every sending domain. SPF/DKIM/DMARC misconfigurations are the #1 cause. Hunter doesn't check this. We do, in one call.

Price at scale for monitoring

If you're monitoring 10,000 customer domains, calling Hunter doesn't make sense — they're not built for that. We are. $49/mo gets you 30k calls/day; that's plenty of headroom for daily monitoring of tens of thousands of domains.

Self-explanatory score

Our 0-100 score + low/medium/high risk band is something you can put on a dashboard without parsing four separate records. Hunter's response is structured for "is this address valid" — a different question.

Where Hunter wins

Email discovery

If your problem is "I need to find the right person to email at company X", Hunter is the answer. We don't do this.

Per-address verification

If your problem is "this specific email address (alex@x.com) — is the mailbox real?", Hunter does an SMTP check. We don't. We can tell you whether the domain is set up to receive email, but not whether a specific mailbox exists.

Tenure + integrations

Hunter has been around since 2015. They have native integrations with every major CRM. We're new.

Which should you use

Use sitetrace if…

You send transactional or marketing email and you need to monitor your sending domain's authentication posture, or your customers' domains if you're an ESP.

Use Hunter if…

You need to find or verify specific email addresses — outbound sales, lead gen, list cleaning.

Use both

Sales team uses Hunter to find and verify addresses. Deliverability team uses sitetrace to monitor the sending domain. Different tools, different problems.
